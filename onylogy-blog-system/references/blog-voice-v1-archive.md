> Onylogy blog post, follow every rule here so the result is indistinguishable from the existing six posts.

## 1. Voice & Tone

- **Second person ("you") is the default.** Talk *to* one beginner, not *about* users. Occasional first person
  ("I'm going to show you", "my coupon code", "How **We** Chose") is fine and welcome — it signals a real person
  behind the post. First-person plural ("we're going to look") shows up in guide-style pieces.
- **Conversational but authoritative.** Friendly, plain-spoken, never stiff or corporate. Explains technical
  ideas without jargon; when a term is unavoidable, it's defined in plain words on first use
  (e.g. "content management system (CMS)").
- **Reassuring.** The reader is a nervous beginner. Remove fear: "Not intimidating." "Plain and simple."
  "That's cool." Confidence-building over hype.
- **Punchy rhythm.** Mix short emphatic one-liners with 2–4 sentence paragraphs. Never wall-of-text.
  Example cadence: *"Fresh out of the box, WordPress is… naked. It needs help to run fast, stay safe, and rank high."*
- **Rhetorical questions to open sections or hook the reader:** "You want people to find your website, right?"
  "Nobody likes waiting for a page to load, right?"
- **Contractions always** (don't, it's, you'll, won't). Writing like speech.
- **Light drama / metaphor for engagement**, especially in plugin/tool roundups — each item gets a persona
  ("The Bodyguard", "The Time Machine", "The Speed Booster", "The Spam Killer"). Keep it tasteful, one per item.

### Signature sentence patterns (reuse these shapes)
- Problem → Solution: "Bad hosting causes slow loading, downtime, and constant frustration." → then the fix.
- Contrast couplet: *"Choose wisely, and everything else becomes easier. Choose poorly, and problems follow you everywhere."*
- Myth-buster: "It's not hype." "That's rare in hosting."
- Reassuring close: "Millions of creators use it because it works — plain and simple."

## 2. Article Structure (the template)

Target length: **~2,000–3,000 words** for pillar/guide posts (What Is WordPress ~2,000; roundups 2,200–3,200).
Shorter listicles (like Hosting) can run **~1,100–1,200 words**. Match length to depth, don't pad.

Standard skeleton, top to bottom:

1. **H1 title** — see §3 rules.
2. **Intro** (2–4 short paragraphs): hook with a rhetorical question or bold claim, state the problem, promise
   what the post delivers. Often includes a big authority stat early ("WordPress powers over 40% of the internet").
3. **Key Takeaways** *(optional but common)* — bulleted summary box, 4 items, bold keyword + short explanation.
   Used on the deeper guides (Mistakes, KadenceWP). Skip on simple listicles.
4. **A "why this matters" / context section** — e.g. "Why the Right Theme Matters", "What Is WordPress Hosting?".
5. **A "how this guide is structured" / "How We Chose" section** on roundups — builds trust/transparency.
6. **Main body** — the numbered list or the topical sections (see §4 for the per-item pattern).
7. **A decision-helper section** — "Which Hosting Provider Should You Choose?", "Final Thoughts — Should You Use it?"
   Maps reader scenarios to recommendations ("On a tight budget: Hostinger or DreamHost").
8. **Conclusion** — H2 named "Conclusion", "Final Thoughts", or "Wrapping It Up". Summarize picks, end on an
   encouraging/CTA note. Often a direct engagement prompt: *"Over to you: Which of these mistakes did you make…?"*
9. **FAQ** — H2 "Frequently Asked Questions" or "FAQ: Common … Questions" / "Common … Questions (FAQ)".
   **Present on all six posts — never skip it.** 6–7 Q&A pairs (KadenceWP had 13). Each answer 1–3 sentences,
   practical, same conversational voice.
10. **"You May Also Like"** internal-links block, then footer (services list, contact, socials). *(Site chrome —
    not something to write, but leave a placeholder note that internal links go here.)*

## 3. Headings

- **H1:** Descriptive + benefit + a parenthetical qualifier aimed at beginners. Title Case.
  - Formula: `[Number/Topic] + [Benefit/Descriptor] + ([Audience or Scope note])`
  - Examples from canon: "Top 6 Hosting Providers for WordPress **(Beginner-Friendly Guide)**",
    "What Is WordPress and Why Should You Use It? **(Beginner Guide)**",
    "Top 6 Free WordPress Themes for Beginners **(Elementor & Gutenberg Picks)**".
  - Roundups use the number **6** consistently ("Top 6", "6 Must-Have").
- **H2:** Either (a) the numbered list item, or (b) a plain-language question/statement. Question-style H2s are
  a signature: "Is WordPress Free?", "What Can You Build With WordPress?", "Which Hosting Provider Should You Choose?".
- **Numbered list items as H2** carry the format: `N. [Name] — [Short Persona/Benefit]`
  - "1. Hostinger — Best Overall Hosting for Beginners"
  - "2. Wordfence Security – The Bodyguard"
- **H3:** Repeating sub-labels inside each item. Keep them **consistent across all items in a post**:
  - Plugins post: "Why You Need It" / "Key Features" / "Alternative:"
  - Mistakes post: "The Issue" / "The Fix"
  - Hosting post: "What makes [X] stand out" + "**Best for:**" line
  - Themes post: "Key Features:"
- Use an em dash (—) or en dash (–) between name and descriptor in headings — the canon uses both; pick one and
  stay consistent within a single post.

---

## 4. Per-Item Pattern (for roundups/listicles)

Every item in a "Top 6 / 6 Must-Have" post follows the **same repeated block** — this consistency is a defining trait:

```
## N. [Product Name] — [One-line persona or benefit]
[Image immediately after the H2 heading]
[1–2 sentence intro to the product]
### [Consistent sub-label, e.g. "Why You Need It" / "The Issue"]
[2–4 short paragraphs]
### Key Features           (bulleted, 3–4 items)
- Feature one
- Feature two
- Feature three
### Alternative: [name]     (or "Best for:" line, depending on post type)
```

Bold the product name on first mention. End tool sections with either an **"Alternative:"** or **"Best for:"** line.

---

## 5. Formatting Conventions

- **Bold** is used deliberately, not decoratively. Bold:
  - Product/theme/plugin names on first mention (Kadence, GeneratePress, Hostinger)
  - Key terms and definitions ("content management system (CMS)", "plugin bloat")
  - Statistics ("43–44% of all websites", "60,000 free plugins")
  - Menu paths ("Settings > Permalinks", "Post name")
  - Critical takeaways / the one line you want remembered
- **Italics** for soft emphasis inside a sentence ("a _huge_ percentage", "_why millions choose it_").
- **Bulleted lists** for features, benefits, requirements, scenario-based recommendations, and Key Takeaways.
- **Numbered lists** only for genuine sequential steps (e.g. the 4-step update routine). Otherwise bullets.
- **Tables** are rare — used once (KadenceWP performance comparison). Use only when comparing specs head-to-head.
- Keep paragraphs to **2–4 sentences max**. Single-sentence paragraphs are allowed and encouraged for emphasis.

---

## 6. Images

- **One image per major section** — placed **immediately after the H2 heading** (roundups: one screenshot per
  item; guides: one illustration per major concept). Roundups therefore have ~6 images; concept guides ~3–4.
- Images are **product screenshots** (homepages, settings screens, pricing tables) for tool/host/theme posts,
  and **conceptual illustrations** for guide posts.
- **Alt text is descriptive and keyword-aware**, following this pattern:
  - `"[Product] homepage showing beginner-friendly WordPress hosting plans"`
  - `"WordPress SEO plugin settings screen for optimizing titles and meta descriptions"`
  - `"Beginner's guide to WordPress – what WordPress is and why to use it for websites"`
  - Formula: **[subject/product] + [what's shown] + [beginner/WordPress context keyword]**.
- Captions (when present) emphasize **features/function, not aesthetics**.
- A **featured image** sits at the top of every post (below H1), with alt text summarizing the whole article.

---

## 7. Links & CTAs

- **Internal links** to sibling posts, woven into sentences naturally (e.g. link "Top 6 Free WordPress Themes"
  and "6 Must-Have Plugins" from the Mistakes post). Always cross-link related canon posts.
- **A mid-article CTA link** is common: "View which hosting will be best for you".
- **External links** to official sources: the product's own site, WordPress.org, Trustpilot, performance tools.
- **Affiliate/discount CTAs** appear where relevant, phrased personally and low-pressure:
  *"Get 10% Discount on any hosting plan. You can use my coupon code: hosten"*. No aggressive selling; no visible
  affiliate disclaimer block in the canon (add one if the client later requires it).
- **CTAs are soft, not pushy** — "check out the features", "audit your website today", never "BUY NOW".
- Close many posts with a **direct engagement prompt** inviting a comment/action.

---

## 8. FAQ Rules

- Always include. H2 heading, then each **question as an H3** (or bolded), answer directly below.
- **6–7 pairs** is the norm (go up to ~13 only for a big cornerstone piece like KadenceWP).
- Answers: **1–3 sentences**, plain, practical, same voice. No fluff.
- Cover the predictable beginner worries: cost/free?, is it safe/secure?, is it hard to set up?, how does it
  compare to X?, does it affect SEO/speed?, do I need Y?.

# PART II — Search & AI Optimization Layer

> Everything above (§1–9) defines **how Onylogy writes**. This second part defines **how Onylogy ranks** — in
> Google *and* in AI answer engines (AI Overviews, ChatGPT Search, Perplexity, Copilot). The voice never changes;
> these rules sit underneath it. Apply both parts to every new post.
>
> Two audiences now: the **reader** (Part I) and the **machine** (Part II). Write for the reader, structure for
> the machine.

---

## 10. Keyword Strategy (do this BEFORE writing)

- **One primary keyword per post**, chosen query-first (how people actually search), not topic-first.
  - Put it in: H1, the first 100 words, at least one H2, the slug, the title tag, and the meta description.
  - Don't stuff — natural density. If it reads awkwardly, rewrite the sentence, not the keyword.
- **5–10 semantic / secondary keywords** (related terms, synonyms, sub-questions) woven through H2s and body.
  These signal topical depth to Google and give AI engines more ways to match a query.
- **Match search intent.** Beginner "what/why/how/best/top" queries = informational. Keep the helpful,
  non-salesy tone (Google's helpful-content system rewards this; your voice already fits).
- **Slug rule:** short, lowercase, hyphenated, keyword-only. `top-free-wordpress-themes` ✅ not the full title.

---

## 11. E-E-A-T & Experience (highest-impact upgrade)

Google's helpful-content / E-E-A-T emphasis (esp. the **Experience** signal) is the biggest gap in the current
canon. Every new post must **prove a human with hands-on experience wrote it**:

- **Show first-hand use, not just knowledge.** Add at least 1–2 "experience beats" per post:
  *"When I set this up on a client site…", "I tested load times before and after…", "The plugin I actually keep installed is…"*.
- **Use original proof where possible:** your own screenshots (with real data), before/after numbers, a small
  result. Generic stock explanations are the thing AI can already generate — your lived experience is not.
- **Author authority:** byline **Ehasanul Haque**, ideally linked to an author bio establishing WordPress/agency
  credentials. Add a one-line author context on cornerstone posts.
- **Accuracy & trust:** every statistic gets a **source and a date** ("As of 2025, WordPress powers ~43% of all
  websites (W3Techs)"). Unsourced stats are now a liability, not an asset.
- **Freshness:** show a "Last updated" date and genuinely refresh cornerstone posts; recency helps both Google
  and AI engines that prefer current sources.

---

## 12. AEO / GEO — Getting Cited by AI Answer Engines

AI engines lift **self-contained passages**, not whole articles. Structure so any one chunk can be quoted
verbatim and still make sense:

- **Answer-first paragraphs.** Open each section (and every FAQ answer) with a **self-contained 40–60 word direct
  answer**, THEN expand in your conversational voice. The first sentence should answer the H2's implied question
  without needing the sentence before it.
  - Example: H2 "What Is WordPress Hosting?" → first line: *"WordPress hosting is web hosting configured
    specifically to run WordPress — with the right server settings, security, and one-click installs built in."*
    Then continue in your usual style.
- **Definition sentences.** Give AI a clean "X is a Y that does Z" line for every key concept. These get cited constantly.
- **Extractable formats win citations:** add **at least one comparison table** per roundup (specs/price/best-for),
  use bulleted lists for criteria, and put standalone stat lines AI can lift.
- **Question-based H2s** (you already do this) map directly to prompts people type into AI — keep and expand them.
- **Semantic completeness:** cover the obvious follow-up questions in the body + FAQ so the AI finds the whole
  answer on your page and cites you as the complete source.
- **Be quotable and specific.** Vague filler never gets cited; concrete claims, numbers, and named tools do.

---

## 13. Structured Data (Schema) — claim the rich results

Every post ships with JSON-LD schema (this is free SERP + AI real estate the canon currently skips):

- **`Article`** (or `BlogPosting`) on every post — headline, author (Ehasanul Haque), datePublished, dateModified, image.
- **`FAQPage`** built from the FAQ section — you already write the FAQ, so this is close to free; can win the FAQ rich result and feeds AI directly.
- **`HowTo`** when a post has genuine sequential steps (e.g. a setup routine).
- **`BreadcrumbList`** for site structure.
- Keep schema content **matching the visible page** (no mismatches — that risks penalties).

---

## 14. Internal Linking — Pillar & Cluster

Move from ad-hoc cross-links to a deliberate hub-and-spoke:

- **Pillar (hub):** broad cornerstone post (e.g. "What Is WordPress").
- **Clusters (spokes):** narrower posts (hosting, themes, plugins, mistakes) that **link up to the pillar**, and
  the pillar **links down to each spoke**.
- **Anchor text is keyword-rich and descriptive** — link the phrase "beginner-friendly WordPress hosting", not
  "click here". This passes topical relevance and helps both Google and AI understand your site's authority map.
- 3–6 relevant internal links per post, plus 1–3 authoritative external links (official docs, W3Techs, etc.).

---

## 15. On-Page Meta Spec

- **Title tag** (~50–60 chars): includes the primary keyword near the front; can differ from H1 to fit length
  and add a hook. Front-load the keyword.
- **Meta description** (~140–160 chars): primary keyword + a benefit + a soft reason to click. Written in-voice.
- **One H1 only** (the article title). Logical H2 → H3 nesting, no skipped levels.
- **Image SEO:** descriptive keyword filenames (`kadence-theme-customizer.png` not `IMG_2043.png`), compressed
  for speed, descriptive alt text (already covered in §6), lazy-loaded.
- **Core Web Vitals matter:** keep images light and layout stable — page speed is a ranking factor and the canon's
  reader-first tone is wasted on a slow page.
