## B. Bangladesh — victim statistics
- **CCAF, Cybercrime Trends in Bangladesh 2024** (132 victims, Apr 2023–Apr 2024; released 29 Jun 2024 at DRU; research head Obaidullah Al Marjuk). TBS 2024-06-29 https://www.tbsnews.net/bangladesh/crime/one-ten-cybercrime-victims-bangladesh-affected-pornography-study-887306
  - women 59% of victims; 78.78% aged 18–30; under-18 13.65%
  - hacking of social media/online accounts 21.65% (top); pornography-related ~1 in 10 (11.35%), rising 5 yrs; overall rate doubled to 11.85%
  - social defamation 47.72%; financial loss 40.15%; "almost all" mental agony
  - only 12% sought legal remedy (81.25% GD, 18.75% written complaint); of those, only 12.50% satisfied
  - education: HSC 40.90%, graduate 21.21%, SSC 16.66%, below SSC 12.87%
- **CCAF 2023 edition** (cited in Siddiqua 2024): ~80% of victims felt they did not get desired help from police.
- **Police Cyber Support for Women (PCSW)** — launched 16 Nov 2020 by Police HQ; all-women officers. Prothom Alo Eng 2025-01-29 https://en.prothomalo.com/bangladesh/n09z2iw96x : 2024 = 9,117 complaints (peak Sep 979, Oct 881); cumulative 16 Nov 2020–31 Dec 2024 = 42,642 complaints. Daily Star 2026-04-17 https://www.thedailystar.net/news/bangladesh/rights/news/laws-exist-online-violence-against-women-continues-4153711 : 60,808 women *sought assistance* inception→May 2024; complaint types doxxing 41%, account hacking 18%, blackmail 17%, impersonation 9%, cyberbullying 8%; NETZ Bangladesh: >78% of women in eight districts faced tech-facilitated violence; quotes: Md Shafiqul Islam (Addl Commissioner, DB, DMP) "when we try to proceed through the legal process, we often do not get cooperation from victims"; Fauzia Moslem (President, Bangladesh Mahila Parishad) "There are hardly any visible examples of perpetrators being punished, and that lack of accountability emboldens others". Prothom Alo article: PhD study 2021-22 — perpetrators ex-boyfriend 33%, online friend 20%, stranger 16%, ex-husband 12%, friend 8%, colleague/classmate 6%, relative 4%; victims 90% urban; 37% students; one-third of complainants disengage; AIG (Media) Enamul Haque: "Police cannot pursue action without formal complaints". Official page https://www.police.gov.bd/en/police_cyber_support_for_women
- **ActionAid Bangladesh 2022 study** (359 women 15–35, six districts; released 27 Nov 2022). Dhaka Tribune https://www.dhakatribune.com/bangladesh/299185/study-63.51%25-of-women-in-bangladesh-face-online : 63.51% faced online violence (50.19% in 2021); platforms FB 47.60%, Messenger 35.37%, Instagram 6.11%, IMO 3.06%, WhatsApp 1.75%, YouTube 1.31%; types: hateful sexual comments 80.35%, explicit images/solicitation 53.28%, discrimination 19.71%, fake-ID harassment 17.47%, stalking 16.16%, attacked for advocacy 13.10%, non-consensual photo sharing 11.79%, sexual-assault threats 11.79%, blackmail w/ intimate photos 2.62%, pornographic editing 1.75%; only 14.91% filed formal complaint; of complainants 64.71% saw no action; 75.77% want anonymous reporting; impacts: 65.07% psychological trauma/depression/anxiety, 42.79% lost confidence online, 25.33% trauma, 24.89% loss of self-dignity; 56.55% never saw an awareness campaign.
- **ActionAid–Prothom Alo 2021 survey** (269 women via Facebook). Daily Star 2021-11-24 https://www.thedailystar.net/news/bangladesh/crime-justice/news/77pc-women-victims-online-abuse-2902411 : 77% sexist/misogynistic comments; 50.37% explicit images in inbox; 30.37% identity theft/fake profiles; 23.70% non-consensual intimate photos; 19.26% cyberstalking; 3 in 4 depression/anxiety; 1 in 10 attempted suicide/self-harm/PTSD; 30.37% reduced online activity; only 15.56% reported to police; of non-complainants 47.41% "complaint does not help", 30.17% didn't know where; quotes: Taslima Yasmin (Assoc. Prof. Law, DU), Khushi Kabir (Nijera Kori), Sara Hossain (BLAST).
- **Sheikh, Hossan & Menih (2023)**, *Journal of School Violence* 22(2):198–214, DOI 10.1080/15388220.2023.2168681 — online survey 552 university students (315 M / 237 F, 18–31), 1–30 Nov 2020: 13.6% cyberbullied in past 12 months; 2.4% perpetrated; deception most common form; no sig. gender difference in victimisation (M 35.36 vs F 34.10) but males sig. more perpetration (29.65 vs 26.53, p<.001); impacts: stressed/anxious 24.5%, angry 21.2%, couldn't concentrate on study 31.8%, uneasy sharing on social media 30.1%, scared to come to campus 9.7%; help-seeking: friends 52.2%, family 24.2%, police 8.3%, university 6.5%, voluntary org 2.4%.
- **Noor et al. (2025)**, *BMC Psychiatry* 25:783, DOI 10.1186/s12888-025-07234-z — BAHWS 2019-20 nationally representative, 4,984 female adolescents: 8% cyberbullied in past 12 months; 12% MDD overall; among cyberbullied, MDD 31%; AOR 3.97 (95% CI 3.12–5.05); dose-response.
- **Mallik & Radwan (2020)**, *Asian Journal of Psychiatry* 48:101893, DOI 10.1016/j.ajp.2019.101893 — 32% of 276 adolescents (10–17) in Dhaka experienced cyberbullying; 27% of victims had a psychiatric disorder.
- **Nova, Rifat, Saha, Ahmed & Guha (2019)**, "Online sexual harassment over anonymous social media in Bangladesh", ICTD '19, Ahmedabad, ACM, pp. 1–12, DOI 10.1145/3287098.3287107 — survey 291 + interviews 27; widespread sexual harassment on anonymous social networks; link between communal culture and anonymous harassment; insufficient infrastructural support for victims.
- **Klose & Jebin (2024)**, *Information Technology for Development* 30(2):246–263, DOI 10.1080/02681102.2023.2279326 — narratives of three Dhaka women; "women's online presence is controlled by patriarchal norms"; social media "as a male-dominated space … promote restrictive and unequal gender stereotypes"; women "pretend to be an ideal woman" to avoid abuse.
- **Siddiqua, Rukhsana (2024)**, "Challenges Faced by Police Officers in Investigating Cyber Crime: An Exploratory Study in Bangladesh", *IJHSSE* 11(7):150–161, DOI 10.20431/2349-0381.1107014 — Dhaka cyber tribunal 2,669 cases 2013–Oct 2020; 768 disposed; 22 convictions = 2.86%; ~80% victims dissatisfied with police (CCAF 2023); eight cyber tribunals in eight divisions since 2021; challenges: lack of training/equipment, victim non-cooperation, anonymity, fake SIM registration, digital evidence complexity.
- **Dhaka Tribune 2019-04-20** https://www.dhakatribune.com/bangladesh/nation/174757/3%25-conviction-rate-of-cybercrime-in-bangladesh : Dhaka Cyber Tribunal Feb 2013→early 2019: 15 convictions of 495 disposed (~3%); cases 3 (2013), 33 (2014), 152 (2015), 233 (2016), 568 (2017), 925 (2018).
- **BenarNews 2021-03-16** https://www.benarnews.org/english/news/bengali/cyber-tribunal-03162021210045.html : 3,324 cases filed 2013–4 Mar 2021; 128 completed; 30 convictions.

## C. Law (all verified on bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd)
- **সাইবার সুরক্ষা আইন, ২০২৬** (২০২৬ সনের ৮১ নং আইন), ১০ এপ্রিল ২০২৬; s.1(2) deemed effective 21 May 2025; repeals & re-enacts সাইবার সুরক্ষা অধ্যাদেশ, ২০২৫ (২০২৫ সনের ২৫ নং অধ্যাদেশ, ২১ মে ২০২৫). http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-details-1710.html
  - **s.25** যৌন হয়রানি, ব্ল্যাকমেইলিং বা অশ্লীল বিষয়বস্তু প্রকাশ: covers blackmailing, sexual harassment, revenge porn, CSAM, sextortion, incl. AI-generated/edited content; s.25(2) up to 2 yrs / ৳10 lakh; **s.25(3) against a woman or child <18: up to 5 yrs / ৳20 lakh / both**. http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-1710/section-57966.html
  - **s.46**: s.25 offences are আমলযোগ্য (cognizable) but **জামিনযোগ্য (bailable)** and **আদালতের সম্মতিসাপেক্ষে আপোসযোগ্য (compoundable)**.
  - s.8 power to remove/block content; s.10 digital forensic lab; s.45 time limit for case disposal.
- **সাইবার সুরক্ষা অধ্যাদেশ, ২০২৫** s.50 repealed **সাইবার নিরাপত্তা আইন, ২০২৩ (২০২৩ সনের ৩৯ নং আইন)**, enacted 13 Sep 2023, which had repealed **ডিজিটাল নিরাপত্তা আইন, ২০১৮ (২০১৮ সনের ৪৬ নং আইন)**, 8 Oct 2018. DSA had absorbed **s.57 of তথ্য ও যোগাযোগ প্রযুক্তি আইন, ২০০৬ (২০০৬ সনের ৩৯ নং আইন)**. Cyber Tribunal constituted under ICT Act s.68 (Dhaka tribunal from Feb 2013).
- **পর্নোগ্রাফি নিয়ন্ত্রণ আইন, ২০১২** (২০১২ সনের ৯ নং আইন, ৮ মার্চ ২০১২) s.8(2): defaming/extorting/mentally torturing via pornography → up to 5 yrs rigorous + ৳2 lakh; s.8(3) supplying pornography via internet/mobile → up to 5 yrs + ৳2 lakh. http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-1091/section-41881.html

## D. Global
- **Plan International, Free to Be Online? (State of the World's Girls 2020)** https://plan-international.org/publications/free-to-be-online/ — survey of 14,000+ girls & young women aged 15–24 in **22 countries** (qualitative work brings total to 31 countries): 58% harassed online; regions: Europe 63%, Latin America 60%, Asia-Pacific 58%, Africa 54%, North America 52%; platforms where harassment happens: Facebook 39%, Instagram 23%, WhatsApp 14%; types: abusive/insulting language 59%, purposeful embarrassment 41%, body shaming 39%, threats of sexual violence 39%, sexual harassment 37%, stalking 32%, racist comments 29%, anti-LGBTIQ+ 26%, threats of physical violence 21%; 47% of harassed girls threatened with physical or sexual violence; perpetrators: strangers 36%, anonymous users 32%, non-friend social media contacts 29%, school/work acquaintances 23%, friends 21%, group of strangers 16%, current/former partner 11%; effects: lower self-esteem/confidence 42%, mental/emotional stress 42%, physically unsafe 24%, problems with friends/family 19%, problems at school 18%, job trouble 7%; 18% of very-frequently harassed stop posting opinions; Philippines 68% (Plan PH press release 2020-10-16); Australia 65% (Plan Australia).
- **Economist Intelligence Unit (2021), Measuring the Prevalence of Online Violence Against Women** (4,561 women, 51 countries, May 2019–May 2020; released 10 Mar 2021) https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/85-of-women-have-witnessed-harassment-and-online-violence-finds-new-research-from-the-economist-intelligence-unit-301244311.html ; UNESCO https://www.unesco.org/en/world-media-trends/measuring-prevalence-online-violence-against-women : 38% personally experienced; 85% witnessed; 65% know a targeted woman; Gen Z/Millennials 45% vs Gen X/Boomers 31%; 54% of attackers known to victim; 32% reconsidered posting, 30% reduced presence, 20% left platforms; 25% reported to platforms, 14% to offline agencies; 92% harm to wellbeing; 35% mental-health issues. (Regional figures 98% Middle East, 91% LatAm, 90% Africa, 88% Asia-Pacific, 76% N. America, 74% Europe = experienced OR witnessed — say so if used.)
- **UN Women / UNFPA definition of TF-GBV**: "any act that is committed, assisted, aggravated or amplified by the use of information communication technologies or other digital tools which results in or is likely to result in physical, sexual, psychological, social, political or economic harm or other infringements of rights and freedoms" — UN Women, Expert Group Meeting report: Technology-facilitated violence against women: Towards a common definition (2023) https://www.unwomen.org/en/digital-library/publications/2023/03/expert-group-meeting-report-technology-facilitated-violence-against-women ; UNFPA https://www.unfpa.org/TFGBV

## E. Definitions / theory (verbatim quotes verified)
- **Kaplan & Haenlein (2010)**, "Users of the world, unite! The challenges and opportunities of Social Media", *Business Horizons* 53(1):59–68, DOI 10.1016/j.bushor.2009.09.003 — "a group of Internet-based applications that build on the ideological and technological foundations of Web 2.0, and that allow the creation and exchange of User Generated Content" (p. 61).
- **Merriam-Webster**: social media = "forms of electronic communication (such as websites for social networking and microblogging) through which users create online communities to share information, ideas, personal messages, and other content (such as videos)" https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/social%20media
- **Belsey, Bill** (cyberbullying.ca / billbelsey.com): "Cyberbullying involves the use of information and communication technologies to support deliberate, repeated, and hostile behaviour by an individual or group, that is intended to harm others." Coined the term c. 2003–04. https://billbelsey.com/?p=1827
- **Hinduja & Patchin**: "willful and repeated harm inflicted through the use of computers, cell phones, and other electronic devices" — *Bullying Beyond the Schoolyard*, 2nd ed., Corwin 2015; https://cyberbullying.org/what-is-cyberbullying
- **UNICEF**: "Cyberbullying is bullying with the use of digital technologies. It can take place on social media, messaging platforms, gaming platforms and mobile phones." https://www.unicef.org/end-violence/how-to-stop-cyberbullying
- **WHO**: "Gender refers to the characteristics of women, men, girls and boys that are socially constructed. This includes norms, behaviours and roles associated with being a woman, man, girl or boy, as well as relationships with each other." https://www.who.int/health-topics/gender
- **Oakley, Ann (1972)** *Sex, Gender and Society*, London: Temple Smith, Introduction (p. 16): "'Sex' is a word that refers to the biological differences between male and female: the visible difference in genitalia, the related difference in procreative function. 'Gender' however is a matter of culture: it refers to the social classification into 'masculine' and 'feminine'."
- **Suler, John (2004)** "The Online Disinhibition Effect", *CyberPsychology & Behavior* 7(3):321–326, DOI 10.1089/1094931041291295 — abstract: "While online, some people self-disclose or act out more frequently or intensely than they would in person." Six factors: dissociative anonymity, invisibility, asynchronicity, solipsistic introjection, dissociative imagination, minimization of authority.
- **Citron, Danielle Keats (2009)** "Law's Expressive Value in Combating Cyber Gender Harassment", *Michigan Law Review* 108(3):373–415 — abstract: online harassment of women "impedes women's full participation in online life, often driving them offline, and undermines their autonomy, identity, dignity, and well-being"; recognising it "for what it is—gender discrimination". **Citron (2014)** *Hate Crimes in Cyberspace*, Cambridge, MA: Harvard University Press.
- **Walby, Sylvia (1990)** *Theorizing Patriarchy*, Oxford: Basil Blackwell — patriarchy = "a system of social structures and practices in which men dominate, oppress and exploit women" (p. 20); six structures: paid work, household production, culture, sexuality, violence, the state.
- **Hirschi, Travis (1969)** *Causes of Delinquency*, Berkeley: University of California Press — social bond: attachment, commitment, involvement, belief.
- **Becker, Howard S. (1963)** *Outsiders: Studies in the Sociology of Deviance*, New York: Free Press — "deviance is not a quality of the act the person commits, but rather a consequence of the application by others of rules and sanctions to an 'offender'" (p. 9).
- **Goffman, Erving (1959)** *The Presentation of Self in Everyday Life*, New York: Doubleday Anchor — front region / back region. **Goffman (1963)** *Stigma: Notes on the Management of Spoiled Identity*, Englewood Cliffs, NJ: Prentice-Hall — stigma = "an attribute that is deeply discrediting" (p. 3).
- **Durkheim, Émile** *The Division of Labour in Society* (1893) / *Suicide* (1897) — anomie.
- NU textbooks (page placeholders): শেখ মোহাম্মদ আবু শামীম ও মোসাঃ পারভীন সুলতানা, *সামাজিক সমস্যা*, ঢাকা: লেখাপড়া, ফেব্রুয়ারী ২০১৩; মোহাম্মদ রফিকুল ইসলাম ও মুহাম্মদ জাকির আল ফারুকী, *সমাজবিজ্ঞান পরিচিতি*, ঢাকা: লেখাপড়া, পুনর্মুদ্রণ জানুয়ারি ২০১৩.
